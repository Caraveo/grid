# A Letter to Miners

*This letter is part of GRID’s social contract with the people who will power the network. It is written in plain language on purpose.*

---

To the miners—

To the ones who learned power draw before breakfast, who know the sound of a healthy fan curve, who built rigs in garages and warehouses and bedrooms while the rest of the world slept—

This is for you.

You already did the hard part. You bought the GPUs. You fought availability. You optimized joules. You stayed online when it was boring and when it was brutal. Bitcoin taught the planet that strangers can secure a network with machines and incentives. Ethereum’s GPU era taught a generation how to scale parallel hardware at home. Then the ground shifted: ASICs took hash, proof-of-stake took the merge, and a lot of honest iron was told—quietly—that its best days were behind it.

**They were wrong.**

GRID is useful mining. Not a puzzle that burns power to prove you burned power—**real work**: AI jobs, frames, simulation, and yes, the future where competitive games and live worlds need milliseconds, not marketing slides. Your hardware still thinks in parallel. The world is starving for that.

So keep mining.

Point the same machines at Proof-of-Resource. Run the node. Set your power limits. Set your heat limits. Keep your primary PC as your primary PC if you want—GRID respects autonomy. When the network verifies your work, you earn **GRID**. Sell it for dollars. Sell it for BTC. Sell it for ETH. Stack it. You already understand markets; we just plugged your craft into a market that buys **compute**.

If you have one card in a small form factor—you belong here.  
If you have a rack that used to hum for ETH—you belong here.  
If you run a datacenter and want to sell surplus capacity—you belong here too.

We will not build a network that only loves warehouses. Datacenters can join; they make the pie bigger. But little miners stay in the design: sharded jobs, a protected inclusion reward pool, low bonds for small nodes, and—when play gets competitive and latency gets ruthless—**edge machines near real humans** become the prize, not the consolation.

Imagine ranked night. Matchmaking lands a lobby across a city. The tick can’t wait for a continent. Somewhere in that metro, a miner who never stopped caring about uptime is close enough to matter. That is not nostalgia. That is architecture.

You are not “idle capacity.”  
You are not a footnote in someone else’s cloud.  
You are how a planetary computer stays human-scale, geographically real, and hard to capture.

The future will ask for more than bigger clusters. It will ask for **trustworthy power, everywhere**—for creators, for labs, for games where a millisecond is the difference between clean and cheated by physics.

Bring your GPUs. Bring your discipline. Bring the culture that already knows how to run a network from the edge of the map.

Mine useful work.  
Get paid in GRID.  
Cash out how you want—**Bitcoin is the Transact Security Layer**: hard settlement for the value you earned.

The supercomputer is not a building you are locked out of.  
**It is the mesh we build together.**

Welcome home.

— GRID
